How this publication works

A buyer's guide is only as good as its honesty. Here's exactly how The Crisp Counter earns money, verifies facts, and stays out of the "AI slop" business.

How we make money

The Crisp Counter is an Amazon Associate — we earn from qualifying purchases. When you click a product link marked "sponsored" and buy on Amazon, we may receive a commission. That's the entire business model. Nobody pays for placement in our guides or rankings, and no brand sees a draft before it publishes.

Every affiliate link is labeled in plain words, tagged with rel="sponsored" for machines, and covered by the disclosure strip that sits under the masthead on every page — plus a repeat in every footer. This mirrors the FTC's guidance on clear and conspicuous material-connection disclosure (FTC Endorsement Guides FAQ) and the Amazon Associates Program Policies.

How product facts are verified

  1. Each product link was resolved to its canonical official listing on 2026-11-20.
  2. Titles, categories, quoted specs, and (for verification) prices, ratings and availability were read directly from those listings — not from memory, not from third-party roundups. Prices, star ratings, review counts and availability are verified but deliberately not displayed on this site: the Associates IP License (§2(b)) limits pricing and Product Advertising Content to API-obtained data, and Participation Requirements §6(t) limits customer reviews and star ratings to PA-API content (recorded in FLG-044).
  3. Every value that appears in a table carries a link to its source row or listing. Anything we couldn't confirm is left blank and labeled unverified — see the comparison table.
  4. Prices and ratings are snapshots. They age. Every display is stamped with its observation date.

The complete audit trail lives in the source register and the product-intent record.

What we never claim

  • No performance claims we haven't measured. We don't test products in a lab. Capacity, accuracy, and preset counts are the seller's published claims — clearly framed as such.
  • No fake reviews, no invented experiences. Ratings we show are Amazon's displayed aggregates, linked to their source.
  • No revenue forecasts or conversion scores. The campaign ledger accepts only figures from authorized first-party reports.
  • No popularity claims about our topics. Guide topics are editorial hypotheses, labeled as such, until measured query data exists.
  • No silently fixed data. When a listing contradicts itself, we publish the contradiction and flag it (see Flagged for Review).

How the machinery stays trustworthy

Parts that can be automated are automated: a CI script (tools/check_site.py) verifies on every change that disclosures exist on every page, that every sponsored link carries its rel tag, that every data row is source-linked or flagged, and that no internal link is broken. Parts that can't be automated sit behind explicit gates: Associates link testing, disclosure sign-off, and the analytics endpoint each require a named human action before their claims go live — see the compliance gate.

Editorial independence

The lineup was defined by the project brief, and we say so. Our guides deliberately discuss trade-offs and "who it's not for," because a publication that only praises isn't useful — and readers can tell. When the day comes that testing data exists, it will come with its method attached.

Contact & corrections

Spotted a stale price or a fact we got wrong? Open an issue on the repository (sassnarep3-star/WinA) referencing the page and the row — corrections are tracked publicly in the methodology log.
