About Counter Space

What this site is, what it is not, and the one limitation you should know before trusting anything here.

The short version

Counter Space is a small buying guide for kitchen appliances that take up counter space. It currently covers three air fryers and two thermometers, chosen because those were the products we were asked to write about.

It is reader-supported. When you buy through a link here, we earn a commission from Amazon. That does not change what you pay.

What we have not done

We have not tested these appliances. We have not owned them, cooked in them, or measured them. No page on this site says otherwise, because saying otherwise would be a lie, and the FTC is explicit that you cannot describe experience with a product you have not tried.

So what is here instead? Writing about the decision rather than the verdict. What a two-drawer air fryer is good at and what it is bad at. Why a 26-quart oven and a 6-quart drawer are not competitors. When a connected thermometer is the wrong tool. That kind of thing can be written honestly from published specifications, and it is often more useful than a star rating anyway.

What we could not show you

Prices, star ratings, review counts and product photographs are all absent. This is not laziness. Amazon's program policies prohibit displaying customer ratings, reviews or current prices unless they are supplied through its own API, and prohibit price tracking outright. We do not have API access. Printing a price we saw once would produce a number that is wrong within days, and printing a rating would breach the terms.

Every specification is marked with how confident we are in it: verified from the official listing, stated by the maker but unverifiable by us, unverified, or withheld. Where a value could not be confirmed, the field is empty rather than filled with something plausible.

How we make money

Affiliate commissions from Amazon. Every outbound product link on this site is marked as sponsored, and the disclosure appears above the content on every page rather than buried in a footer.

We do not accept payment for placement. We do not rank products by commission rate, and we could not do so honestly even if we wanted to, because we have not verified the current commission rate for either category — it is an open question in our register.

Corrections

If a specification here is wrong, it should be fixed rather than defended. The register page lists every source used, with the date it was read, so any claim can be checked directly.

What is still unresolved

Plenty. A page listing every open question, and what would settle each one, is published at Open questions. It is not a marketing page. It is the actual list.

How this page is designed, and what it will not do

No Amazon product images, no stock photography of other appliances, and no manufacturer press files are used anywhere on this site. Program Content may not be stored or hot-linked outside approved tools, so every visual here is original artwork made for Counter Space and captioned as an illustration. It shows food and counters, never the products for sale, so nothing in a picture can be mistaken for the item a link leads to.

A second rule is written into the build: the phrases that give a generated marketing page away are banned here, and the build fails if one appears. Not because any of them is illegal — because a reader who has seen that marketing voice forty times stops believing anything else on the page.

Banned in every reader page, enforced by _src/build.mjs: “elevate”, “elevated”, “game-changer”, “game changer”, “revolutionize”, “revolutionary”, “seamless”, “seamlessly”, “curated”, “unleash”, “unlock the”, “dive into”, “delve”, “in today”, “must-have”, “top-notch”, “cutting-edge”, “state-of-the-art”, “next level”, “embark”, “tantalizing”, “tantalising”, “a testament to”, “symphony of”, “look no further”, “got you covered”, “beginner or a pro”, “whether you are a beginner”, “best in class”, “best-in-class”, “industry-leading”, “effortlessly”, “transform your kitchen”, “take your cooking”, “ultimate guide”, “miss out”, “act now”, “limited time”, “at the end of the day”, “in conclusion”, “synergy”, “holistic approach”.

A third rule is arithmetic. Where a figure can be drawn honestly it is drawn to scale from the register — the counter footprint, the stated capacity — and where a manufacturer has not published the number, the diagram shows the gap rather than a shape invented to fill it. The build compares every drawn number with the specification row it cites and stops if they disagree, so an illustration cannot survive a data correction by being wrong in a flattering direction.

The design brief this build works from

  1. DB-01

    Use an opinionated editorial type system: a serif display face for headlines and a clean sans for body, set in a clear hierarchy with generous line height and a readable measure.

    Constraint: None. Keep contrast and a readable line length, especially on mobile.

  2. DB-02

    Use a restrained, limited palette with one accent color. Avoid generic blue-to-purple gradients and neon highlights.

    Constraint: WCAG contrast.

  3. DB-03

    Do not use emoji or icons as decoration. Use a real icon set only where it carries meaning.

    Constraint: None.

  4. DB-04

    Vary section layouts: a strong editorial hero, a reading column for guides, and at least one non-card treatment per page. Do not repeat the identical card grid in every section.

    Constraint: Mobile-first; keep tables and cards readable.

  5. DB-05

    Keep the disclosure strip and sponsored labels exactly where policy requires, and design around them rather than treating them as an afterthought.

    Constraint: FTC Disclosures 101 (hard to miss); Associates Operating Agreement §5.

  6. DB-06

    Write in a concrete, human, opinionated voice using specifics and named trade-offs. Ban filler phrases and generic marketing boilerplate.

    Constraint: FTC: no experience you do not have. The Associate statement stays verbatim.

  7. DB-07

    Until compliant product imagery is available, keep the site typographic: color, type and a defined illustration or pattern system carry the design. Do not hot-link, store, or substitute Amazon imagery, and do not use stock photos of a different appliance.

    Constraint: Participation Requirements §6(a); IP License. See FLG-007.

  8. DB-08

    Make the sponsored status of every outbound product link visible at the link, and keep the Associate statement above the fold on every page.

    Constraint: FTC 16 CFR 255.5; Associates OA §5; Program Policies.

The brief is a working document on the suggestions page, where every recommendation carries the decision recorded for it and the date it was taken. What the site could not buy from here is flagged rather than quietly dropped: FLG-007 records that there is no licensed route to product imagery until the Product Advertising API is approved, and the illustration direction is recorded as an open question for the owner (Q-26, FLG-034).
