The decision board

A decision list, not a wish list. Each suggestion states what to do, why, what it costs, and what would make it a bad idea. Nothing here has been implemented beyond what is already live; the ones marked shipped were built in this pass because the brief asked for them directly. Everything else waits for a decision. The 2026-11-20 editorial pass (arena-27f76c22) added SUG-25 to SUG-29 and shipped SUG-25; SUG-25 and SUG-23 deliberately record opposite positions on seasonal content so the owner can choose with both arguments visible. Each suggestion can also be adopted, modified, held or rejected on the decision board above; the choices export as text, and nothing is implemented before a choice is handed back to the build.

SUG-01 · weight 10 · shipped

Keep the site in 'how to decide' voice, permanently, until products are actually tested

Why

The FTC forbids describing hands-on experience you do not have, and readers detect the fake version instantly. The reliable way to sound human without lying is to write about the decision instead of the verdict: what the drawers are like, what the counter cost is, who the machine is wrong for.

What it costs

None. It is a writing constraint, enforced by the build.

What could go wrong

A guide that never says 'we tested this' competes poorly against sites that can. That is a real disadvantage and it is the correct one to accept.

Recommendation

Accept. Every page already follows it.

SUG-02 · weight 9 · recommend

Split into two publications, or pick one category and go deep

Why

Three air fryers and two thermometers are two different buying moments. Search engines reward a site that owns one topic. A small site trying to own 'kitchen gear' owns nothing.

What it costs

Splitting means two domains, two sitemaps, twice the setup. Going deep on air fryers means the thermometers get a single supporting page.

What could go wrong

If the thermometer affiliate offers a better commission or a bounty, narrowing too early gives up real money.

Recommendation

Recommend: keep one site, make air fryers the front section, and treat thermometers as the accessory section — which is what the current structure already does. Confirm before adding a third category.

SUG-28 · weight 9 · hold

Buy traffic before conversion measurement exists

Why

Paid acquisition is reasonable only once two things are true: the collector is deployed (FLG-011) so clicks per source are countable, and the Associates tracking report confirms attribution (FLG-004). Neither is true today. Spending before measurement means paying for clicks nobody can trace to an order.

What it costs

Budget, plus the two blockers above.

What could go wrong

The failure mode is not overspend — it is confidence: unmeasured paid traffic looks identical to organic growth in every report the site currently has.

Recommendation

Hold until FLG-011 and FLG-004 close. Re-evaluate in the week after the first Associates report arrives.

SUG-05 · weight 8 · recommend

Publish on a real cadence: a new guide every week for eight weeks, then read the query data

Why

Question-shaped guides are the right shape, but five is not a sample. A weekly cadence for two months produces enough pages and enough query impressions to see which question people actually arrive with, which is the only way to stop guessing topics.

What it costs

A substantive guide per week. Each needs its own verified spec pass.

What could go wrong

Cadence without quality control produces the thin content the brief explicitly rejects. The build gate must stay on.

Recommendation

Adopted 2026-11-20 as the publishing cadence; see the decision log. A new guide every Monday (New York time) for eight weeks. Basis, checked on Amazon Associates Help: content should be "recent (generally within the last 60 days)" and "a good rule of thumb is at least 10 posts" (FLG-013). Each guide is added to the sitemap and feed in the same commit and must pass the build and compliance gates. Measurement at week eight needs Search Console and analytics, which are owner-blocked (FLG-009, FLG-011); it is not estimated.

SUG-26 · weight 8 · recommend

Grow the technique cluster by one article a month — jobs, not verdicts

Why

Technique questions (what to reheat, what to batch, what belongs on the counter) are evergreen, answerable from published specifications, and never require claiming a hands-on test. They are also the cheapest way to close the Associates 10-post bar (FLG-013): this pass added the technique and holiday guides, leaving roughly three additional substantive posts to reach ten on any reading.

What it costs

A guide per month plus its verification pass — the same cost as the weekly cadence in SUG-05 and SUG-21, at a slower, sustainable rate.

What could go wrong

Ten guides already cover form factor, capacity, cleanup, multi-function, thermometers, technique, and holidays. A new article must be a new job (reheating, defrost-and-crisp, batch-prep) or it will restate the same trade-offs and read as filler — the exact failure the brief rejects.

Recommendation

Recommend. Start next month if the weekly cadence (SUG-05, SUG-21) is not adopted; if it is, slot technique articles into the same calendar. Each new guide enters the sitemap and feed in the same commit, per the standing rule.
