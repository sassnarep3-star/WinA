Disclosure

Affiliate disclosure

The full statement, in the plainest terms available, plus the rules that shape what this site can and cannot show.

The required statement

As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.

That sentence appears under the masthead on every page of this site, and again in the footer. It is required by section 5 of the Amazon Associates Program Operating Agreement, and it is not negotiable wording.

What it means for you

If you click a product link on this site and then buy something on Amazon, we may receive a commission. You pay the same price you would have paid anyway. The commission comes out of Amazon's margin, not your pocket.

Not every click earns anything. A commission only applies if a purchase happens within the window Amazon sets, and Amazon decides what qualifies.

How we mark links

Every outbound product link is marked in three ways:

  • A visible "Sponsored" label or a plain-language note next to the link.
  • The rel="sponsored" attribute in the markup.
  • A link that goes directly to a visible amazon.com address, so the destination is never hidden.

Amazon's policies require that it be clear you are linking to Amazon, and that the destination not be obscured. We do not route product links through a redirector or a shortener of our own.

What we are not allowed to show, and do not

  • Star ratings and customer reviews. Prohibited without Creatives or Product Advertising API access.
  • Prices and price history. Prices must come from Amazon's API to stay accurate, and price tracking or alerting is prohibited outright.
  • Amazon logos and Program Content. Not used anywhere on this site.

Independence

This site is not endorsed by, sponsored by, or affiliated with Amazon beyond participation in the Associates program. No brand mentioned here has reviewed or approved any page. We do not imply otherwise, and Amazon's operating agreement forbids implying otherwise.

Editorial independence, honestly stated

There is a real tension in affiliate publishing: the site earns when you buy, and the most useful advice is sometimes "do not buy this." We handle it by writing about trade-offs instead of winners, and by saying plainly which product is wrong for which person. That is not a guarantee of objectivity. It is the structure we can actually defend.
