About

A shop that shows its sums

Buying guides for small appliances, built on the listing, the manual, and the public record — never on a hype cycle. The Preheat covers small kitchen appliances — air fryers, multi-cookers, and the two square feet of counter that decide whether either gets used. It is independent and reader-supported.

Why this exists

Most affiliate pages about appliances are a paraphrase of a listing with a superlative bolted on, arranged so that whatever pays best looks best. You can feel it within a paragraph and you can never check it, because there is nothing to check against.

We started from the opposite constraint: write only what a reader can verify, and publish the list of what we could not. That is why this site has a register of sources with dates and quotations on it, why it has no star ratings, and why the shelf currently has locks on it instead of opinions.

What we can legitimately tell you

Which numbers to ask an appliance for. What the trade-offs actually are when you live with a machine for a month. What the government food-safety chart says about temperatures, verbatim, with a link. Where the arithmetic of running cost comes from. What an affiliate disclosure has to say and where it has to sit.

What we will not tell you

That anything is the best. That we used it. That the price is X. That a thousand owners averaged out to four and a half stars. If you came for those, this is the wrong tab, and the right one is the retailer's own page.

How to read a page here

  1. The title is the question. The first paragraph answers it. You may stop there.
  2. Anything numerical is either quoted with a source link or computed live from your own input.
  3. A dash in a table means "could not confirm", never "zero" and never "unknown but probably fine".
  4. The what we did not check box at the end is the part to read first if you are deciding whether to trust the rest.
