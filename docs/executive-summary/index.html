Executive summary

Where this programme stands, and what it is waiting on

A reader-facing publication now exists, with its compliance rules written as code and its product data deliberately empty. The commercial half of the brief — the part that turns traffic into commission — is specified, gated, and blocked on one answer only: five real ASINs. Nothing else in the plan is waiting on anybody.

1 · The verdict

Ship the publication, hold the monetization. The site is worth publishing now: it answers real buyer questions, it can be checked line by line, and it reads like something a person edits rather than something that was generated. It is not yet worth submitting to an Associates review, because the product identifiers supplied for it did not survive a one-line format check, and a review of an empty-shelf site with malformed links is a rejection that cannot be re-assessed.

That is the whole tension in this project, and it is worth naming plainly: the marketing layer is ready and the product layer has no products. Everything else in the brief — the cadence, the clusters, the gate, the analytics, the ledger — is downstream of that and already built to receive the data.

3 · The one blocking finding

All five submitted identifiers — B04G1MEwY, B02NbDxp3, B00hJ57DV, B0c8moW59, B0f3GKalj — are 9 characters. Amazon's published definition of an ASIN is a unique 10-character alphanumeric code, typically beginning B0, and the checkable place to read one is the product page URL after /dp/. The host supplied alongside them, link.amazon, is not an Amazon product host, and no Associates tag appears in the URL — the tag has to appear as a parameter in the link for the referral to be attributable.

Consequence, precisely: not "the links are invalid" but "no listing has been identified". The register therefore holds 45 blank fields across five records, and every product card on the site renders a locked control with the reason printed. No title, price, rating, availability, category or commission figure was written anywhere, because none could be confirmed. Guessing the tenth character would have been the single most damaging thing this build could have done: it would have produced plausible-looking errors attached to a marketing claim.

4 · Compliance posture

  • Disclosure is structural. The Operating Agreement's exact sentence appears in a strip beneath the masthead on every page and again in the footer, with a plain-language line above the first product control on any page that has one. The gate verifies presence and order, so a future template edit cannot quietly move it below the buy buttons.
  • Every paid link is qualified. Any Amazon product link the build emits carries rel="sponsored nofollow" and a visible (paid link) label; editorial citations to policy documents carry no tag, so nothing looks like a referral when it is a reference.
  • No implied endorsement. Nowhere does the site suggest Amazon supports, sponsors or verifies it — that prohibition sits in the same clause as the disclosure and is checked as a phrase ban.
  • No incentives, no bookmark prompts, no direct-from-elsewhere links, all three of which the Program Policies rule on, and all three implemented as lint rules rather than as advice.
  • No fabricated experience. The FTC standard this site is built to satisfy is not only disclosure but honesty: an endorsement must reflect the endorser's genuine opinion, and results claims must not stand in for the typical experience. Nothing was tested, so nothing claims to have been.
  • Customer questions go to Amazon. The contact page states it explicitly, because the agreement bars an Associate from handling Amazon customer contacts.

Two clauses are quoted from a readable locale copy of the agreement rather than the US text and are marked as such in the register (see /verify/, read-level column). They inform the lint rules; they are not printed to readers as quotations. Re-read them before any legal sign-off.

5 · Measurement posture

The brief asked for click and conversion measurement before further writing, and that ordering is right — with one correction that changes the design: conversion is not measurable on this site. Orders happen on Amazon, attribution is Amazon's to report, and the only authorized source for what happened after the click is the Associates reporting you can log into. So the site measures the half it can see, precisely:

  • page_view — once per document, after the disclosure strip has painted. Informs: which guide earns arrival; decides the next topic alongside Search Console queries.
  • product_card_impression — when a product card is at least half visible for one second. Informs: whether the reader scrolls to the shelf at all — separates a copy problem from a placement problem.
  • outbound_click — on any Amazon link, captured before navigation so the count does not race the unload. Informs: click-through rate by slot; the only honest input to deciding where the buy button belongs. Never carries a per-user identifier..
  • gate_block — when a reader clicks a product control that the compliance gate has locked. Informs: how much demand the verification backlog is actually costing — the number that justifies clearing FLG-001 first.
  • newsletter_signup — on submit of the reader-capture form, only when data/forms.json enabled=true. Informs: whether a reader wants a second email; carries no address, which stays only in the chosen processor.

Storage is aggregate-by-day by construction: no cookie, no browser identifier, no IP retained, no sub-tag attached to a person. That last one is not a privacy preference, it is a Program Policies prohibition on associating Associates reporting identifiers with individual end users — a design choice a lot of "affiliate analytics" tooling gets wrong by default.

What is not running: the endpoint needs a runtime, GitHub Pages serves static files, and no page on this site claims that analytics are live. Until a host exists, the site publishes the counter's absence rather than a placeholder number.

6 · Editorial posture

Six guides, all question-shaped, all ending with the same short list of what was not checked. They are written for the reader who is close to buying and is trying to work out which of three listings differs in a way they would notice — which is a different audience from the one that wants a verdict handed to it. Capacity is treated as inner dimensions rather than quarts; cleanup as a weekly habit rather than a coating; multi-function as an arithmetic of trade-offs rather than a count of presets.

The four topics the brief proposed as working titles are published as guides, and labelled on the page as hypotheses rather than as demand: nothing on the site says a subject is popular, because no query data exists yet. The first genuinely measured topic choice becomes possible after two weeks of Search Console data, using the tool that reads the export (see /measurement/).

Two structural choices are worth flagging upward: authorship is a desk byline with an explicit "AI-drafted, human-edited, nothing tested" note, and imagery is labelled illustration. Both are honest, and both are one decision away from being better — a named consenting editor (Q-07) and your own photographs of the products (Q-12) are the two highest-trust upgrades available, and neither needs a line of code.

7 · Money, and the refusal to forecast it

There is no revenue projection in this project, in either direction. Not "expect a few hundred a month", not "this niche averages 3% conversion". A forecast assembled without a listing corpus, without query data, and without a single tracked click would be fiction in a table, and the site's whole differentiator is that its tables can be checked.

What exists instead: the ledger with its columns and its allowed-source list; the two-week cadence for filling it; and a rule that any derived figure (EPC, orders per 100 clicks) prints a dash while any input is null. That is the discipline the brief asked for — measure before automating optimization — expressed as a validator rather than as an intention.

8 · What the automation does, and does not

The brief describes an autonomous platform: research products, spot trends, analyse price and reviews and competition and commission, generate SEO articles, publish and manage links, track clicks and conversions, continuously optimise. That is a coherent system. It is also a system with two parts that cannot be delegated: confirming a product exists as described, and deciding that a claim is safe to print.

10 · The next 14 days, and then 90

Days 1–14 · make it real

  1. Answer Q-01, Q-02, Q-03 (one login, one copy-paste).
  2. Run npm run collect on the first pasted listing, confirm the register fills without a single inferred field.
  3. Build one tagged link per product; click it logged out; confirm the destination, then confirm the click in reporting the next day.
  4. Unset the lock in config only after that test passes for all five.
  5. Verify the property in Search Console and submit the sitemap.

Days 15–90 · make it compound

  1. Publish on the chosen cadence; every guide into the sitemap and feed automatically.
  2. At ~100 clicks per slot, let the click data move the buy button, not a hunch.
  3. First topic chosen from a query export rather than from the brief.
  4. Shoot your own photos of whatever you buy, and retire the illustration labels.
  5. Re-read the register; close or escalate every flag older than the review interval.

These are commitments, not forecasts. No line above should be read as an expected outcome; the exit criteria for each path are on /suggestions/, and they are all checkable states rather than revenue figures.
