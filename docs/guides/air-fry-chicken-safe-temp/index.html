Air-frying chicken: the one check that matters more than the recipe

Small convection ovens brown the outside fast and can leave the middle behind. Here is the number, the tool, and where to put it.

By The Preheat desk · published Nov 20, 2026 · last reviewed Nov 20, 2026 · 5 min

Published because it is the one question in this cluster we can answer from an official government source instead of from opinion. Drafted with AI assistance, edited by the desk. No product was tested for this guide — and no human credential is implied by the byline.

We have not tested any appliance for this guide. It is a checklist you can finish, not a verdict you have to trust. Why we publish like this.

An air fryer is a small fan-forced oven with a very short distance between the element and the food. That is why the outside of a thigh goes mahogany while the joint near the bone has barely reached the temperature anyone asked for.

The number

ProductMinimum internal temperature and rest time
All poultry (breasts, whole bird, legs, thighs, wings, ground poultry, giblets and stuffing)165 °F (73.9 °C)
Ground meats160 °F (71.1 °C)
Beef, pork, veal and lamb steaks, chops and roasts145 °F (62.8 °C), rest at least 3 minutes
Fish & shellfish145 °F (62.8 °C)
Leftovers165 °F (73.9 °C)
Casseroles165 °F (73.9 °C)

Source: USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service, Safe Minimum Internal Temperature Chart. The agency's own framing: "Cook all food to these minimum internal temperatures, as measured with a food thermometer, before removing food from the heat source." Consumers may cook to higher temperatures for preference. Page last updated Apr 14, 2025; table reproduced here as read on Nov 20, 2026.

Why the appliance does not get a vote

Air fryer recipes are written for taste, and taste lives in browning. Temperature guidance lives in safety. When the two conflict, the thermometer wins, and the honest thing to do is set a longer time and check earlier than the recipe tells you, rather than trusting that a 400 °F blast for twelve minutes reached the middle of a large thigh.

  • Pull the biggest piece, not the most convenient one.
  • Avoid bone. Bone conducts and flatters the reading.
  • For stuffed bird, the centre of the stuffing has to reach 165 °F too — the chart counts stuffing in the poultry line.
  • Reheating last night's tray? Leftovers are a 165 °F item, not a "until it's hot" item.

What this means when you are buying

Three things on a spec sheet turn into cooking safety, in order of how much they matter here:

  1. Single-layer area, because crowding is how a centre stays cold.
  2. Whether you can see the food without stopping the cook, because the check happens mid-flight, not after.
  3. How easy the basket is to open with one hand while holding a thermometer in the other. Genuinely. It is the difference between checking and hoping.

Thermometer advice, free of charge and of commission. Any instant-read thermometer you will actually keep in a drawer does the job. A cheap one you use beats a good one that is in a cupboard. If you buy nothing from this site, buy that.

What we did not do. We are not a food-safety authority and we are not quoting one beyond the table above, which is reproduced from the USDA FSIS page so you can check it yourself. For anything the chart does not cover — smoking, drying, sous vide, the exact thickness of your thighs — go to the source for that subject, not to a buying guide.

Short answers

What temperature is chicken done?

Cook all poultry — breasts, whole bird, legs, thighs, wings, ground poultry, giblets and stuffing — to a minimum internal temperature of 165 °F (73.9 °C), according to the USDA FSIS Safe Minimum Internal Temperature Chart.

Is a colour check enough?

No. Colour is not a reliable indicator of safety, which is why the guidance is to measure with a food thermometer.

Where do you put the thermometer in an air-fryer basket?

In the thickest part of the largest piece, avoiding bone, without touching the basket. If you have to check three pieces, check the one that was most crowded.

Why do air fryers undercook the middle?

Fast surface browning plus a crowded drawer: air cannot move between pieces, so the outside sets before the centre comes up. Cooking in a single layer and checking the thickest piece is the fix.
