Basket or oven-style air fryer: what should you compare?

Basket-style and oven-style air fryers cook and fit counters differently. Here is what to compare before you choose, using only listing text and official references.

By Editorial team · Published 2026-11-20 · Last checked 2026-11-20

Start with the shape of the food you cook

Most air fryers fall into two shapes. A basket-style unit has a drawer with a perforated basket, and food sits in one layer that you shake or flip. An oven-style unit, sometimes sold as an air fryer toaster oven, has a door, racks, and usually a pan. It takes larger or odd-shaped loads.

Neither shape is better in general. Compare them with what you cook most often. If your usual batch is a few portions of fries, wings, or vegetables, a basket is often enough. If you want to roast a tray of vegetables, bake a small loaf, or cook two things at different settings, an oven-style unit gives you more room to work.

We did not test any of these units, so we cannot say how they cook.

Three questions to answer before you compare prices

  1. Does the unit take the food you actually cook? Think about a normal week, not the best-case recipe.
  2. Where will it sit? Measure the space, including room to pull out a basket or open a door. Our check did not capture product dimensions, so use the listing's product details.
  3. How many things do you need to cook at once? A dual-basket unit can cook two foods, but the listing gives each basket's size, not one total.

Read the claims as claims

Titles include claims such as "450°F Hi-Fry Option." These are manufacturer claims. We have not checked them. If a temperature matters for your food, follow the manual and check the result with a food thermometer. The USDA minimum cooking temperatures are in our thermometer guide.
