Questions to ask before buying a multi-function kitchen appliance

Eight functions is a number, not a benefit. This is the argument you should have with yourself before the box arrives.

By The Preheat desk · published Nov 20, 2026 · last reviewed Nov 20, 2026 · 8 min

Topic from the project brief's working titles. No demand or superiority claim is made about any appliance type. Drafted with AI assistance, edited by the desk. No product was tested for this guide — and no human credential is implied by the byline.

We have not tested any appliance for this guide. It is a checklist you can finish, not a verdict you have to trust. Why we publish like this.

Multi-function appliances are sold on the breadth of the list and judged by the depth of one thing. Before you buy, ask which one thing you will actually use on a Wednesday, and whether that one thing is the reason the machine exists.

The seven questions

  1. Which function do I already own? If the box does slow-cooking and you own a slow cooker you like, you are buying a duplicate with a warranty.
  2. Which function replaces a device I use weekly? That is the only honest justification for paying for breadth.
  3. How good is it at the one thing, measured by the thing's own constraints? For pressure: PSI and pot diameter. For air frying: basket area and whether the crisp lid is removable so the pot is usable.
  4. What does the lid weigh and where does it go? A lid you must balance on a handle is a lid that ends up in the sink for a month.
  5. Which parts touch food, and are they sold separately? Seals, valves, baskets, pots. Consumables you cannot buy are a plan to throw the machine away.
  6. What does the manual say about the warranty and who services it? Two years from a brand with no parts channel is a shorter warranty than one year from a shop on your street.
  7. What happens when the software changes? Read whether presets can be updated, and whether the appliance still works without the companion app.

The arithmetic of breadth

Every extra function costs something that does not appear on the feature list: a button you will never press, a menu you have to scroll past, a seal that has to survive being used both as a steam gasket and a crisp-lid gasket. Buy the shape that matches the two or three jobs you actually do.

Running cost, since the fan runs longer than you think

A small countertop oven used 45 minutes a day at a stated 1,500 W is a different bill from an oven used for the same job, but you cannot answer that from marketing copy — you need the wattage from the listing and the rate from your own supplier. Put both in and let the arithmetic be yours.

Two safety lines worth reading in full

First, from the appliance manual: how to release pressure, what the vent must never be covered by, and what "do not fill past" means with foam-producing foods. Second, from the food-safety side: an appliance that cooks quickly is not exempt from the temperature check. The USDA FSIS chart asks for a minimum internal temperature of 165 °F (73.9 °C) for all poultry and 165 °F for leftovers and casseroles, measured with a food thermometer before the food comes off the heat.

What we did not do. We did not pressure-cook anything for this guide. We are not telling you that any category wins. What we are telling you is that the seven questions above cost nothing and decide the purchase.

Short answers

Is a multi-cooker worth it if I mostly want an air fryer?

Probably not. A machine that does both well is usually a pressure cooker with a crisp lid bolted on, and the crisp step is slower and smaller than a dedicated unit. Buy the appliance for the job you do weekly.

What is the most-overlooked spec?

The inner pot. Its material, its diameter, and whether replacements are sold separately decide more of your daily experience than the number of presets.

Do I need the app?

No. An app is a second thing to update and a place for a manufacturer to change behaviour you liked. If a preset only works with it connected, that is a design decision to notice before you pay.

Will it smell like last night's curry?

Silicone seals hold aroma. Most units ship with more than one ring for exactly this reason, and the second one is usually not in the box.
