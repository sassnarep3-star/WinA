How to assess cleanup and counter-space trade-offs

Cleanup and counter space decide whether an air fryer stays out or goes back in a cabinet. Here is how to judge both from the listing and your own kitchen.

By Editorial team · Published 2026-11-20 · Last checked 2026-11-20

Cleanup depends on what touches the food

Baskets, trays, racks, and the cooking chamber all collect grease and crumbs. The question is how easily each part comes clean.

  • Look for "dishwasher safe" in the listing. Two titles in our set include the phrase: Chefman 6 Quart Dual Basket Sponsored ("Dishwasher Safe Frying Baskets") and Chefman Air Fryer 8 Qt TurboFry Sponsored ("XL Nonstick Dishwasher-Safe Basket"). The title for Emeril Lagasse 26QT French Door Sponsored does not mention dishwasher safety, so check its accessory list before you assume anything.
  • Count the parts. The Emeril Lagasse 26QT French Door Sponsored title says "Six Cooking Accessories Included." Six parts can mean six things to wash.
  • Check how the nonstick surface is described. Both Chefman 6 Quart Dual Basket Sponsored and Chefman Air Fryer 8 Qt TurboFry Sponsored list a "Nonstick" basket. The listings do not say how long the coating lasts, and no coating claim is verified here.

Counter space is more than the footprint

  • Footprint: the width and depth of the base.
  • Clearance: room to pull out a basket, or to open a door fully. Basket units need space to slide the drawer out. Oven-style units need room for the door.
  • Storage: whether it fits in a cabinet when you are not using it.
  • Venting: where it can release heat. Follow the manual for minimum clearances.

A simple test before you buy

  1. Measure the spot: width, depth, and height under any cabinet.
  2. Find the unit's dimensions in the listing's product details. Our check did not capture them, so do not assume a figure.
  3. Compare the two, leaving room for the door or drawer.
