How to assess cleanup and counter-space trade-offs

Appliances are abandoned for friction, not for faults. Sixty seconds of arithmetic about your own sink predicts month four better than any rating.

By The Preheat desk · published Nov 20, 2026 · last reviewed Nov 20, 2026 · 6 min

Topic taken from the project brief's working titles; no claim is made that this is a searched-for subject. Drafted with AI assistance, edited by the desk. No product was tested for this guide — and no human credential is implied by the byline.

We have not tested any appliance for this guide. It is a checklist you can finish, not a verdict you have to trust. Why we publish like this.

There are two questions nobody asks at the checkout. Where will this live, and who is washing it. Get them wrong and a good appliance becomes a bulky object you stop using by the second month.

Counter space: three numbers, one of them obvious and ignored

  1. Its footprint — width × depth, from the listing.
  2. Its clearance — the gap the manual demands at the back and above. Most people read this line, nod, and shove the unit flush against a wall.
  3. Your reach — how high you are willing to lift a hot drawer. The counter under a wall cabinet at 54 inches is a different decision from an island.

Add 1 to 3. If the sum exceeds what you have, the machine is not too big for your kitchen; your kitchen is too small for that shape, and a slower, smaller, duller model may be the right buy.

Decide who washes

It sounds like a joke and it is the actual variable. If the dishwasher is a shared, contested machine in your house, a "dishwasher safe" claim is not a solution; it is a queue. In that kitchen, the appliance you will still be using is the one with the fewest parts, not the one with the cleverest coating.

Write the honest sentence down: this will be washed by hand, on Tuesdays, by me. If reading it is unpleasant, do not buy the multi-part machine, whatever it can crisp.

Small tricks that genuinely move the needle

  • A silicone mat or liner under the food turns a scrub into a rinse, at the cost of browning underneath.
  • A splash of water in the bottom of a drawer while it is still warm stops baked-on grease at source. Test with it off and cool first, on a model you know the housing of.
  • Store the drawer out of its housing so the seam can dry, which is the specific thing that goes mouldy.
  • Keep the manual's cleaning page open in your notes on day one. It answers the "can I put this in the dishwasher" question with more authority than any review does.

What we did not do. No hands-on wash test, no photo of a greased basket, no measured difference between two coatings. This is a checklist, not a lab result. That is deliberate: we would rather you finish it in your own kitchen than trust ours.

Short answers

Where should an air fryer go on a small counter?

Wherever the hot air exhaust has room and the cord reaches. If that means a shelf or the floor of a cabinet on cooking day, that is a legitimate answer, not a compromise — as long as the surface is not something that warms up or melts.

Is it worth buying a silicone liner?

If your weekly failure is the basket, yes. Liners trade crispness on the underside for a rinse instead of a scrub, and cheap ones curl up into the airflow. Try one before you commit to a model because you assumed you would hate washing it.

How do I stop a counter appliance becoming clutter?

Give it a permanent home that does not need clearing, and a storage place for the accessories. If the machine has to be lifted out of a cupboard each time, it will stop being lifted out.
