What temperature should your meat thermometer confirm?

The USDA publishes minimum internal temperatures for each type of food. Here is the chart, what it does and does not tell you, and how the two thermometers on this site are described.

By Editorial team · Published 2026-11-20 · Last checked 2026-11-20

Start with the food, not the thermometer

A meat thermometer is only useful if you know the number to aim for. The USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service publishes minimum internal temperatures for each food. Its guidance says to cook all food to these temperatures, "as measured with a food thermometer, before removing food from the heat source."

FoodMinimum internal temperatureRest timeSource
Beef, pork, veal and lamb steaks, chops and roasts145°FAt least 3 minutesUSDA FSIS
Ground meats160°FNot statedUSDA FSIS
All poultry, including ground poultry165°FNot statedUSDA FSIS
Fish and shellfish145°FNot statedUSDA FSIS
Eggs160°FNot statedUSDA FSIS
Leftovers165°FNot statedUSDA FSIS
Fresh or smoked ham, uncooked145°FAt least 3 minutesUSDA FSIS

The chart also lists reheating temperatures for fully cooked ham. The page is the reference, so use it for the full list.

What the chart does not cover

The chart does not say where to place a probe, how often to check calibration, or how to read a wireless display. Follow the thermometer's own instructions for those.
