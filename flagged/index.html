FLG-033 review

ASIN definition confirmed on an Amazon-owned page; that page says it was AI-assisted

The brief gives 'Amazon's ASIN definition' as the reason the intended-ASIN field is checkable. Earlier searches on 2026-11-20 found only a Seller Central forum thread, which is not cited. A later search found Amazon's own 'Sell on Amazon' page, Understanding ASINs (sell.amazon.com, dated 4 June 2025; source id amazon-asin-definition in data/policy.json). Both chunks were read on 2026-11-20. It defines an ASIN as "a unique 10-character alphanumeric code assigned to every product listed in the Amazon store", says the format "typically starts with 'B0'", and says the ASIN appears after '/dp/' in the product URL. It supports one check only: each observed ASIN is 10 characters, A-Z and 0-9, and each observed destination ends in /dp/<ASIN> (data/links.json). It does not show which product a submitted short link was intended to reach, so the intended-ASIN field stays blank. Two caveats keep this at review: the page is seller-education material, not an Amazon Help article or Associates policy; and the page itself says it 'was produced with the assistance of generative artificial intelligence (gen AI)'. Kept at review rather than resolved for that reason, so the owner can accept it or supply a human-written Amazon Help page.

What we did: Owner: accept this AI-assisted Amazon page as the definition source, or find an Amazon Help page for it. Whether the intended-ASIN field may be filled from a submitted link is still the owner's decision (FLG-019), and it must not be filled from the observed destination.

FLG-013 action

Content volume is below the Associates review bar

Amazon's Associates Help, 'Application Review Process' (checked 2026-11-20, US help topic G8TW5AE9XL2VX9VM), says: "All Sites must have robust original content (even when advertising is removed) – a good rule of thumb is at least 10 posts." It also says: "Content on your website must be recent (generally within the last 60 days)." This site has 5 guides and 5 product pages. Amazon does not say whether product and hub pages count as posts, but 5 guides is below the rule of thumb on any reading. The account's own status and review history are unknown from here. So the review timing depends on qualified sales, not only on content; the action below is unchanged.

What we did: Do not submit or re-submit for review until the site has at least 10 posts and content dated within the last 60 days. The weekly cadence in the suggestions register (SUG-05) is the proposed path. Recorded as a commercial blocker, not a content-quality one.
