Five questions to ask before buying any small kitchen appliance

Not air-fryer questions. Appliance questions. Ask them of a pocket thermometer and a 26-quart countertop oven alike, and most buyer regret disappears before you click buy.

Counter Space editorial 6 min read No hands-on testing claimed

The short answer

Every guide on this site exists because one of these five questions went unanswered. They are written as a checklist you can use on any countertop appliance — including the two thermometers and three air fryers in our register, which we use as worked examples rather than as recommendations.

On this page

  1. Where exactly will it live?
  2. What does the manufacturer claim versus what does the listing claim?
  3. Which SKU am I actually buying?
  4. What does ownership cost that the price does not show?
  5. What is the warranty, and what do I have to do to keep it?
  6. What we cannot answer for you

1. Where exactly will it live?

Not "on the counter". Which stretch of counter, and what does it displace. A footprint is two numbers and a height, and it is often published — the Chefman 6-quart dual basket states 13.38 × 11.81 × 10.63 in; the Emeril brand page states 16.9 × 15.8 × 14.8 in — but it is usually buried below the food photography, so search the spec block for it before you decide.

If the published listing has no dimensions, that is information too. Our register leaves the Chefman 8-quart's dimensions blank and marked unverified, because the brand publishes dimensions for a matte-black 8-quart SKU (RJ38-8T-BLACK) while this listing describes stainless steel, and borrowing numbers across SKUs is exactly how a spec table becomes fiction.

2. What does the manufacturer claim versus what does the listing claim?

They are different documents and they disagree. The ThermoMaven brand page describes the unit as 100% waterproof and publishes no IP code; the Amazon listing title states IP67. Our register keeps both, attributed separately, and warns you not to read the listing's IP number as a manufacturer spec.

Do the same check yourself in about thirty seconds: open the brand's own product page for the model number on the box, and compare. Where the two disagree, believe neither fully — and never let a retailer's bullet points teach you a spec the manufacturer will not sign.

3. Which SKU am I actually buying?

Small appliances come in families: same name, different contents. The CHEF iQ Sense family on Amazon includes a two-probe Gen3 and a three-probe Gen3 with the same marketing copy, plus a limited-edition colourway; the count in the title is the difference, and it is the difference that matters if you cook two things at once.

Same brand, same model line: the Chefman 6-quart dual basket listing and the brand's TurboFry Touch Dual-Window page match on capacity, two 3-quart baskets, Sync Finish, Hi-Fry and dishwasher-safe baskets. The brand page names model RJ38-SQPF-3TDB-2W. The listing title does not name it. Check the model number on the box when it arrives, because the warranty follows the model, not the review count.

4. What does ownership cost that the price does not show?

Three things the box never mentions: parts, filters and counter friction.

  • Parts. Nonstick baskets wear. Before buying, check whether the brand sells replacement baskets — Chefman sells accessories on its own store, which is a real signal about how long the machine stays useful.
  • Cleaning. "Dishwasher-safe" in a listing is a seller's description of a coating, not a guarantee about your dishwasher. Our register marks those rows as claims, not verified facts.
  • Friction. An appliance that needs a preheat, a shake and a sink of washing competes with the oven. If it loses that race, it becomes a storage problem.

5. What is the warranty, and what do I have to do to keep it?

Read the warranty paragraph on the manufacturer's page, not the listing. Two of the five products in our register show why this is a question and not a formality: the Chefman brand page states a one-year assurance, while ThermoMaven's terms are written to run for the life of the unit conditional on registering within 30 days of purchase. A promise you have to activate in the first month is a subscription you forgot to cancel in reverse — and neither of those sentences is a claim we are making about your coverage. The exact wording is on each product's page with the source that publishes it.

Set the reminder the day it arrives. Then answer the earlier questions again: if the counter does not have room, no warranty rescues the purchase.

What we cannot answer for you

Whether you will like cooking with it. Nobody can, and any site that claims to know either tested the product or invented the answer. This site did neither — see how these guides are written.

Takeaways

  • Footprint first: which stretch of counter, and what it displaces.
  • Compare the manufacturer's page with the listing; where they disagree, keep both claims apart.
  • Identify the SKU by model number, not by marketing title.
  • Read the warranty on the brand page — some of them have a deadline.
