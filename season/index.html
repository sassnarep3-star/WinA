The seasonal layer

The same five products, and five different reasons to pick one

Which appliance is right genuinely changes with the calendar — not because the appliances change, but because what you cook, how long for, and how often you wash up change. None of this is measured. It is a reading of the published specifications against how people actually cook.

January

The reset

The month people notice how much counter they are giving away. This is the one season where the cleanup guide matters more than the capacity guide, because the appliance you resent is the one you stop using. The dual basket's two removable baskets are a smaller washing job than an oven's door, rack and crumb tray — and a machine you do not have to scrub is a machine you reach for.

Spring

Grill season opens

The first long outdoor cook of the year is where a thermometer stops being optional. Colour is not a safety indicator — the USDA is explicit that surface browning happens before the interior is safe — so the tool that decides the cook is the one you put in the food. An instant-read handles a chicken breast; a leave-in probe handles the four hours you are not standing there.

Summer

A hot kitchen and no patience

In July nobody wants a 45-minute preheat for two portions, and a large chamber in a hot room is the wrong shape entirely. The small single basket wins on season alone: less metal to heat, less to wash afterwards, and nothing permanently occupying the counter. It is also the season to be honest about what you will actually cook — one batch of one thing.

Autumn

Hosting

This is the season the 26-quart chamber starts to make sense. A maker's own serving guide puts that capacity at food for four to six people — which is a claim about a serving guide, not a guarantee of a single round, but it is the only published number of its kind in this set. A tray that stays flat, a door that opens, and one chamber that does not have to be run twice.

November & December

The big cook

Whole birds, sides, and a cooker that has to hold one large thing in one layer. The question that decides it is the one nobody publishes: the interior cavity dimensions. Measure your bird against the cavity opening before you order, and pick the thermometer based on who is going to take the temperature during the cook — not after.

Why no recipes?

Because we did not cook anything in these appliances, and a recipe page written by someone who never ran one is a page of guesses dressed as instructions. What we can do honestly is tell you which appliance is the right shape for the meal, and which published specification makes that true.
