Suggestions

A decision list, not a wish list. Each suggestion states what to do, why, what it costs, and what would make it a bad idea. Nothing here has been implemented beyond what is already live; the ones marked shipped were built in this pass because the brief asked for them directly. Everything else waits for a decision.

SUG-01 Keep the site in 'how to decide' voice, permanently, until products are actually tested shipped 10 The FTC forbids describing hands-on experience you do not have, and readers detect the fake version instantly. The reliable way to sound human without lying is to write about the decision instead of the verdict: what the drawers are like, what the counter cost is, who the machine is wrong for. None. It is a writing constraint, enforced by the build. A guide that never says 'we tested this' competes poorly against sites that can. That is a real disadvantage and it is the correct one to accept. Accept. Every page already follows it.

SUG-12 Do not add a price tracker, price alerts, or a deal feed reject 10 Prohibited outright by §6(y) regardless of how it is built. Zero. It is the most requested feature on affiliate sites and the most direct route to account termination. Rejected. Documented so no future session builds it.
Participation Requirements §6(y)
SUG-13 Do not generate volumes of near-duplicate pages to chase long-tail queries reject 10 Template-spun variants of the same article are the definition of the thin, generated content the brief rejects, and they dilute the pages that could rank. Zero. It works briefly, then gets the whole domain discounted. Rejected. The build gate refuses pages without unique content fields.
SUG-02 Split into two publications, or pick one category and go deep recommend 9 Three air fryers and two thermometers are two different buying moments. Search engines reward a site that owns one topic. A small site trying to own 'kitchen gear' owns nothing. Splitting means two domains, two sitemaps, twice the setup. Going deep on air fryers means the thermometers get a single supporting page. If the thermometer affiliate offers a better commission or a bounty, narrowing too early gives up real money. Recommend: keep one site, make air fryers the front section, and treat thermometers as the accessory section — which is what the current structure already does. Confirm before adding a third category.
SUG-03 Get PA-API or Creatives access before writing another word recommend 9 It is the unlock for the three things this site most obviously lacks: prices, ratings, and product images. All three are currently blank because the rules require API-sourced data. One credential fixes all of it at once. Eligibility requires qualifying sales first — a chicken-and-egg problem for a new account. None to the site. The risk is waiting on access and shipping nothing meanwhile, which is why the current build ships without it. Apply as soon as the account qualifies. Track it in the questions register as Q-11 and Q-12.

Participation Requirements §6(w)
SUG-05 Publish on a real cadence: one guide a week for eight weeks, then read the query data recommend 8 Question-shaped guides are the right shape, but four is not a sample. A weekly cadence for two months produces enough pages and enough query impressions to see which question people actually arrive with, which is the only way to stop guessing topics. One substantive guide per week. Each needs its own verified spec pass. Cadence without quality control produces the thin content the brief explicitly rejects. The build gate must stay on. Recommend a fixed day and a fixed length. Add each new guide to the sitemap and feed in the same commit, then measure at week eight.
SUG-06 Add a real author byline with a named person recommend 8 Unattributed buying advice reads as generated, which is precisely the failure the brief names. A byline with a short bio and a stated method is the cheapest credibility signal available. Requires a real person willing to be named and accountable. A byline attached to content the person did not write is worse than no byline. Decision needed from the requester. Cannot be fabricated.
SUG-07 Move to a custom domain before building any authority recommend 7 A /WinA/ subpath reads as a side project, and moving later discards whatever equity accumulated. Doing it now costs a domain and one setting. Domain registration plus DNS. About an hour. Slight setup friction with HTTPS and the CNAME file. Recommend. The build already keeps the origin in one config value, so the change is small.
SUG-08 Deploy the analytics collector, and accept that orders cannot be counted yet recommend 7 Click data is available today and is the fastest honest feedback loop. Order data has to come from Associates reports, which arrive later. Separating the two keeps the ledger truthful. One worker deploy and a secret. A self-hosted collector is a privacy obligation. The current design stores no identifiers, which keeps that burden small. Recommend deploying to close FLG-011. Do not import a third-party tracker as a shortcut.

SUG-09 Add a 'who should not buy this' block to every product page consider 6 The single most useful thing a buying guide can say, and the thing competitors skip. It also converts better, because readers trust a page that talked them out of something. A short block per product. None. It requires actually understanding the trade-off, which is why most sites omit it. Consider. The current pages carry a trade-off line; expanding it to a full block is the natural next edit.
SUG-10 Build a real comparison table with measured dimensions consider 6 The comparison page exists but is thin because dimensions are unverified. Counter footprint is the number that actually decides a purchase, and nobody publishes it clearly. Researching dimensions from manufacturer spec sheets, with a source link per number. A wrong dimension is worse than a blank. Only add numbers with a source. Consider once manufacturer sheets are gathered. Until then the table shows what is verified and says what is not.
SUG-11 Add a short 'how we choose what to recommend' block to the front page consider 5 Readers arriving cold need one sentence on why these five products and not others. It also pre-empts the 'is this an ad' reflex. Three sentences. Can tip into self-referential process talk, which the brief explicitly wants kept off the front page. Keep it to a single block low on the page. Consider. The About page currently carries this.
SUG-14 Hold the email capture until there is something to send hold 5 Collecting addresses with no ESP and no publishing cadence means collecting nothing. The form is currently inert and says so. Saves an ESP subscription. Capture opportunities are lost while waiting. Hold until SUG-05 produces a cadence. Then wire one ESP and one welcome email.
SUG-15 Hold all paid search hold 5 No bid is permitted on Amazon marks, and a new site with no conversion data cannot tell whether an ad is profitable. Advertising before measurement is guessing with a budget. Zero. Slower initial traffic. Hold until the ledger has real click and order data.
Participation Requirements §6(f)
SUG-16 Publish a dated 'last checked' line on every product page consider 5 Specifications and availability change. A visible date tells the reader how much to trust the page, and forces the site to actually re-check. One field, refreshed on a schedule. A stale date is a public admission of neglect. That is the point. Consider. Pages currently record the observation date in the register rather than on the page.

If you accept only three

SUG-04 — prove one affiliate link actually tracks, because nothing else matters if it does not. SUG-03 — get API access, which unlocks prices, ratings and images at once. SUG-05 — publish weekly for eight weeks, then let the query report choose the next topic instead of instinct.

Two suggestions are rejections

SUG-12 (price tracking) and SUG-13 (generated near-duplicate pages) are listed so that a future session does not build them in good faith. The first breaches Amazon's program policies outright; the second produces the exact content this publication exists to avoid.
