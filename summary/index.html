Executive summary

What was asked for, what exists now, what is missing, and what to do next. Written 2026-11-20.

What was asked

Build an Amazon affiliate site around five submitted product links, with automated research and content generation, SEO, click and conversion tracking, and strict compliance with Amazon Associates policy and FTC disclosure rules. Ship it to GitHub Pages, coordinate through a shared task board, and merge without waiting for approval.

What exists

  • A five-product publication — three air fryers, two thermometers — with individual pages, two category hubs, and a full comparison table.
  • 9 question-shaped guides, written as decisions rather than rankings, including a technique cluster, a sizing cluster and a seasonal cluster.
  • A seasonal collection page carrying the holiday cluster in the navigation, with the calendar-driven (not demand-driven) topic choice disclosed on the page itself and the opposing "hold" position recorded beside it.
  • Publisher imagery — generated counter scenes and diagrams, labelled on-page as illustrations, so no page claims to show a product.
  • A complete source register: 11 primary sources and 60 specification rows, 36 of them filled and each with a verification status; the rest are blank on purpose, with the reason recorded.
  • Marketing copy for all five links — a social post, a newsletter paragraph and its mandatory disclosure per product, on the marketing kit.
  • Ten policy checks traced to the exact clause, with the text quoted verbatim so the summary can be audited.
  • A publication gate in the build: it fails if any page loses its disclosure, or if any Amazon link loses its sponsored marking.
  • SEO foundations: sitemap, RSS feed, canonical URLs, structured data, and descriptions written for humans.
  • A defined measurement plan: five events, a self-hosted collector, and a ledger schema.
  • A review surface: 34 suggestions with their cost and risk, 3 recorded decisions with dates and reasons, and a single decisions page where the current state of every row is visible before anything is implemented.
  • Two job-first service pages — Dinner in one drawer and Hands off the grill — which start from the meal rather than the machine.
  • Original illustration, no borrowed photography: 16 generated SVG assets and two self-hosted OFL fonts, served from this repository. Nothing is hot-linked, and nothing depicts a product it has not measured.
  • 12 documented gaps on open questions, each with the artifact that would close it and who holds it.

What does not exist, and why

This section is the important one.

  • No prices, ratings, reviews or product photos. Amazon's program policies prohibit displaying these without API access. We do not have it. Showing them anyway would risk the account; inventing them would be worse.
  • No hands-on testing. None took place, so none is claimed. Every page writes about the decision instead of a verdict, and the FTC's rule against describing experience you do not have is the reason.
  • No traffic or conversion data. The site launched with this build. Search Console is unverified and the analytics collector is not deployed, so nothing is being measured yet.
  • No automated product research. The brief asked for an agent that identifies trending, high-converting products. Identifying "trending" requires a data source; without one, any such claim would be a fabrication with a chart attached. The pipeline is defined and its data inputs are documented as missing.
  • No revenue projection. Four unknowns multiplied together produce fiction.

What is unresolved

48 items on the flagged for review page. Four matter commercially:

  1. The tracking ID shopnow047c-20 may not be enrolled. If it is not, nothing on this site earns anything.
  2. The compliance status of the submitted link.amazon short links is genuinely unknown — we did not declare them invalid, we simply do not depend on them.
  3. Search Console is unverified, so nobody knows whether the site is indexed.
  4. No analytics collector is deployed, so no learning is happening.

What to do next, in order

  1. Review the suggestions, the draft design brief and the topic roadmap on /suggestions/, and record accept / reject decisions in the decision log before implementing anything.
  2. Sign in to Associates and confirm one product link per product appears in the tracking report.
  3. Deploy the collector in _worker/ and set the endpoint, then rebuild.
  4. Verify Search Console, replace the placeholder token, submit the sitemap.
  5. Publish a new guide every week for eight weeks — the technique cluster proposed in SUG-26 supplies the topics.
  6. At week eight, read the query report and replace the topic hypotheses with measured demand — including whether the seasonal cluster earns its annual refresh (Q-23).
  7. Apply for Product Advertising API access once the account qualifies — it unlocks prices, ratings and images together.

The honest verdict

What exists is a small, well-documented, compliant publication with 9 guides, a seasonal collection, two job-first service pages and 5 products. It is not yet a business: it has no traffic, no verified affiliate attribution, and no data. It is, however, built so that when those three things arrive, nothing has to be torn down — and so that nothing on it is a lie.

The main strategic risk is not technical. It is that 9 guides is still not enough content to rank in a crowded category, and the plan for fixing that is a cadence, not a feature. The decisions blocking the next step are owner calls recorded in the registers: the seasonal cadence (with the opposite position recorded beside it), the generated brand imagery, and — above all — measured demand, which no amount of writing substitutes for.
